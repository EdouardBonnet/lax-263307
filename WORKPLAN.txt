Planned proof branches, following the paper
  1. Separator/treewidth bridge, then the light-separator theorem (Lemma 6):
     vertex-capacitated flow–cut (Lemma 7), repeated cuts (Lemma 8),
     tree-decomposition transfer (Lemma 9), existing subcubic graphs
     (Lemma 10), and the sampled-path contradiction (Lemma 11).
     Randomness is used only in an existence argument; the separator
     algorithm remains deterministic.
  2. The rational relaxation R_r(G,k), indicator feasibility, and entropy
     decrease under conditioning (Lemma 12). Preserve the global mass k.
  3. The existing ellipsoid method with early success, coefficient bounds,
     and recursive separating inequalities for the independent-set hull.
  4. Solve/RoundOrCut: certified upper bounds, approximation loss, termination
     and total quasipolynomial runtime (Lemma 14, Theorem 15, Section 5.3).
  5. Independent blow-ups preserve the separator hypothesis (Lemma 16),
     then rational scaling/rounding and projection of the selected copies
     prove the weighted guarantee in Theorem 2.
  6. Compile/combine the actual RAM programs and prove their execution and
     rational-size bounds, keeping the polynomial term in original bit size
     additive. This is part of the proof, not an assumed cost annotation.

Concept review points
  Balanced separators use the original order n and exact integer inequality
    3|K| <= 2n. The bound c(m+1)^b includes edgeless singleton graphs.
  Rational weights include negative values; preprocessing can drop them.
  Constants and the program depend on the class, not on a particular input.
  No class-recognition algorithm or separator oracle is supplied.
  The explicit sufficient RAM word width is bounded by the same QPTAS
    resource bound. Polynomial bit simulation preserves its asymptotic form.

Proof progress — 2026-10-12
  Completed the relaxation block with no sorry/admit or proof-package axioms:
    - independent-set indicators satisfy all five constraint families;
    - their exact convex hull is contained in the relaxation;
    - the exact hull is nonempty iff the target independent set exists;
    - conditioned costs lie in [0,1], sum to k, fix chosen vertices to one,
      and preserve positive moments at positive-cost steps;
    - the normalized first and second moments satisfy the probability,
      symmetry, marginal, edge-vanishing, and conditional-probability
      identities in equations (15) and (16);
    - independence bounds give valid inequalities on the exact hull
      (Lemma 13);
    - the conditioned-cost test exactly detects violation of that cut.
  These are seven annotated proofs across five theorem concepts, supported
    by LinearRelaxation's definitions and RelaxationAlgebra's used helpers.
  Continued 2026-10-13 with seven more annotated proofs:
    - entropy lies between zero and log_2(n);
    - the average entropy decrease dominates ordered adjacency mass;
    - an excessive edge cost forces a positive-cost step with strict
      entropy decrease greater than 2/p;
    - at most r conditioning steps guarantee a low-edge-cost set
      (the existence assertion of Lemma 12);
    - fixed-order candidate enumeration contains exactly the sets of
      cardinality at most r;
    - there are at most (r+1)(n+1)^r candidates;
    - the executable exact rational search returns a valid conditioning set.
  The entropy proof uses natural-log algebra internally and converts to
    bits. Zero probabilities and zero marginals are handled explicitly.
  The rational test clears the positive conditioning denominator, counts
    ordered adjacent pairs, and requires no entropy evaluations. Candidates
    are generated by prescribed-length sublists rather than by generating
    the full power set. The list count is proved, not a RAM timing claim.
  Next: RAM instruction counts and rational bit-length bounds for this
    enumeration, or another independent branch. The light-separator branch,
    ellipsoid oracle bridge, recursion, and blow-up arguments remain.

Weighted and blow-up progress — 2026-10-13
  Added fourteen more annotated proofs:
    - independent sets lift to all copies and project back independently;
    - lift cardinality, projection capacity, and exact total blow-up order;
    - signed weights reduce to nonnegative weights by clipping, followed by
      removing nonpositive selected vertices;
    - the empty set handles every all-nonpositive instance, including n=0;
    - pointwise floor bounds and total rounding loss on any vertex set;
    - the rounded copy count is at most 2n²/ε;
    - an ε/2 cardinality approximation on the rounded graph projects to an
      ε weight approximation on the original graph;
    - independent blow-up closures (with empty fibres) are hereditary;
    - labeled copy graphs belong to that closure;
    - bounded multiplicities lift a tree decomposition to bags of size at
      most s(w+1), using the registered tree-decomposition definition.
  The weighted guarantee compares with an optimum chosen only for analysis;
    no optimum or separator is supplied to an algorithm. Maximum weight is
    defined as the maximum of all vertex weights and zero, covering empty
    inputs. The positive-weight branch includes the case n=1.
  Zero-copy fibres are allowed. For hereditary host classes this agrees with
    the paper's nonempty-fibre convention after deleting unused host vertices.
  Numeric execution check: weights [5,2,0] and ε=1/2 give maximum 5, scale
    5/12, and multiplicities [12,4,0]. Lifting {0} selects 12 copies and
    projects to weight 5. Clipping [-3,5,0] gives [0,5,0]; filtering all
    vertices leaves total original weight 5.
  The separator-preservation estimate of Lemma 16 still requires its
    high-degree deletion and isolated-vertex construction. The current
    lifted decomposition handles bounded fibre sizes only. Those structural
    steps, the unweighted algorithm, and the full RAM runtime remain open.
